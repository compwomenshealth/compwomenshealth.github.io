Workshop on Computational Methods for Women's Health

February 16, 2027
UC Berkeley, CA

About

Computational methods can help answer some of the most basic open questions in women's health, such as what a healthy menstrual cycle looks like and how to diagnose endometriosis and PMOS earlier. Large-scale data is increasingly available, yet data-driven approaches to women's health have lagged behind other biomedical fields.

We hope to create an academic community of researchers in ML, statistics, biology, and medicine to collaborate and start tackling these open questions.

Goals

  • Convene researchers in data science, CS, and statistics who are working on or interested in women's health
  • Outline the current state of computational women's health research and identify concrete open problems and future priorities

Scope

"Women's health" is broad, so this workshop focuses on conditions that primarily or exclusively affect women, such as:

  • Menstrual physiology
  • Endometriosis
  • PMOS
  • Reproductive health
  • Menopause

Topics like breast cancer, maternal health, and baseline sex differences in conditions such as cardiovascular disease are important but outside the scope of this one-day meeting.

Intended audience

This workshop is invite-only. Attendees will include:

  • Academics (PIs, postdocs, and PhD/MS students) working at the intersection of computation and women's health
  • Clinicians and clinician-researchers, industry researchers (wearables, FemTech, health systems), and funders
  • Researchers already involved or interested in different areas of women's health: reproductive health, menopause, menstrual physiology, endometriosis, PMOS, etc.
